Backway to Crown King & I-17 Corridor Status

Trailhead: Lake Pleasant to Crown King (25.2 Miles, Technical Rating 7 Difficult)
Campfire Ban LIFTED ADOT 511 Active
Trail Status Briefing Updated: Nov 08-10, 2026

The 25.2-mile Backway begins at Lake Pleasant and climbs into Crown King in the Bradshaw Mountains. While the first 11 miles on Cow Creek Road (marked as French Creek Rd) are graded dirt road, recent storms have caused washouts and boulder accumulation:

  • Gold Mine Washout: Road washed out at the active gold mining area; active mining company reroute passes around the backside of the mine with a shallow soft-dirt water crossing.
  • The Waterfall Obstacle: Ledge drop currently measured at 3.0 to 3.5 feet. Short wheelbase rigs and side-by-sides (SxS) require rock stacking. High-centering risk is acute for standard pickups without 3"+ lift and lockers.
  • Boulder Squeeze: Directly following the waterfall; narrow rocky choke point with off-camber slip hazard. Maintain passenger-side line.
  • Transit Duration: Plan 6 to 8 hours for a full traverse. Crown King offers refueling, saloon dining, and lodging at 5,771 ft.
I-17 Corridor Winter Risk Analysis Today: Risk 1/5 Low

Camp Verde to Stoneman Lake Grade: Curvy, steep, and shaded corridor where black ice forms first during cold frontal transitions. No highway services exist between Camp Verde and Munds Park.

Saturday Transition: Pacific system arrives late Friday night with light rain expected around Big Park and Camp Verde by 08:30 MST Saturday (Risk moves to 2 of 5). 4WD required if ascending into Snowbowl access roads.

Aerodynamic Recovery & Meteorological Radiosondes

Fairing Recovery System: Two composite halves separate in vacuum; at ~50,000 ft altitude, a 110 sq ft drogue parachute deploys, followed by extraction of a 3,000 sq ft steerable parafoil. Power boats and salvage ships deploy Rigid-Hulled Inflatable Boats (RHIB) in the offshore Atlantic "Superbox" (>5 nmi offshore) using GPS data recorders and high-intensity strobe beacons.

Upper Wind Radiosondes: Shoe-box sized sensor packages powered by 9V batteries rise on latex weather balloons to 12–19 miles (63,000–100,000 ft) to profile wind shear before bursting into ocean disposal.
